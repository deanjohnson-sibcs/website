EPM Selection

How to choose an EPM platform without vendor bias

Most EPM selection processes are quietly steered by the people who benefit from the answer. Here is how to run one that is not.

There are six or more credible EPM platforms on the market, each strong for different reasons. Choosing the wrong one is a mistake you live with for years and pay for in millions. Yet most selection processes are shaped, subtly, by whoever stands to gain from the decision.

The vendor's pre-sales team runs a beautiful demo of the scenarios their product handles best. The implementation partner who is certified on one platform somehow concludes that platform is the right fit. The analyst report you are leaning on is, in part, funded by the vendors in it. None of this is dishonest, exactly. It is just not neutral.

A good selection removes that bias by design. Here is the shape of one.

Start with your requirements, not the market

Before you look at a single product, get clear on what you actually need. Run short workshops with finance, IT and the business, and separate the genuine requirements from the nice-to-haves and the habits. What does your close really look like? Where does planning break down? Which integrations are non-negotiable? What is the honest state of your data?

Write it down and weight it. The weighting is the important part: a platform that is brilliant at something you do not need should not score well just because it is brilliant.

Score every platform the same way

Use one scorecard, applied identically to every candidate. We weight eight dimensions:

  • Functional fit to your actual requirements
  • Consolidation, close and reporting depth
  • Planning and forecasting capability
  • Data and ERP integration
  • Total cost of ownership, five years out (not year one)
  • Implementation complexity and risk
  • Vendor viability and product roadmap
  • Fit with your team's skills and appetite

The discipline of scoring everyone the same way is what makes the result defensible. When the board asks why platform A won, you can show them.

Run the demos on your terms

Do not watch the vendor's demo. Make them run yours.

Write a small set of scenarios drawn from your real processes, a tricky intercompany elimination, a driver-based reforecast, a month-end you actually struggle with, and ask every vendor to demonstrate those. You learn far more from watching a platform handle your awkward case than from a polished script that avoids it.

Model the real cost

Licence cost is the visible tip. The real number includes implementation, integration, ongoing administration, the specialists you will need to hire or retain, and the cost of change three years in. Model total cost of ownership across the shortlist over five years. It frequently reorders the ranking.

Keep the decision independent

If the person advising you also earns margin on the platform they recommend, you do not have advice, you have a sales process with extra steps. The value of a source-system-agnostic adviser is precisely that there is no right answer for them, only the right answer for you.

That is the whole point of doing this properly: a recommendation your board can stand behind, because it was reached the same way it would have been for any platform, and for no one's benefit but yours.
